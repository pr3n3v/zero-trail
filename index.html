People don't sort. Now the law says they must.

  • Mixed waste loses its value.Once food scraps touch paper and plastic, recyclers pay less for them or turn them away, and more of it gets dumped.
  • Four streams are now mandatory.Since 1 April 2026, every waste generator in India has to separate wet, dry, sanitary and special care waste.
  • Big generators need proof.Large buildings and campuses above the bulk threshold must register on a central portal and file annual returns.
  • Nobody wants to do it by hand.In apartments, offices, schools and public spaces, people simply won't sort their own waste.

What happens inside

  1. 1

    Detect

    A sensor spots your hand and the lid opens. The item lands on the platform.

  2. 2

    See

    Lights come on and the camera takes a photo.

  3. 3

    Decide

    The model on the bin picks wet, dry or other in under a second. If it isn't sure, it picks other.

  4. 4

    Tilt

    The platform tilts 45° left or right and the item slides down a chute. For dry waste it snaps to 90° and drops it straight down.

  5. 5

    Sync

    Counts and fill levels go to ZeroTrail OS whenever there's a connection.

It sorts with no internet, finishes each item in about four seconds, and the three bins pull out for emptying.

Why now, and why Ladakh first

The rules changed

Since April 2026, every generator must separate four streams, and bulk generators must register and file annual returns on a central portal.

Trust broke

Regulators found about 700,000 fake plastic recycling certificates. Brands now need data they can actually defend.

AI got small

A sorting model can now run on affordable hardware inside the bin itself, with no internet connection.

Fragile mountain regions feel the waste problem first and hardest. If the system works in Ladakh, it can work anywhere.
